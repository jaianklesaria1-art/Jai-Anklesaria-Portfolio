Neon Raptor Brewing Co.

Redesigning a Nottingham brewery's website so that ordering beer online feels like walking into their taproom.

Client: Neon Raptor Brewing Co., Nottingham Year: 2025 Role: Research, UX/UI, AI-assisted build Live site: neonrapterbrewery.netlify.app

01

The challenge

Neon Raptor is an independent brewery in Sneinton Market, Nottingham, with its own bar next door, the Rap Taproom. Their brand is a whole world: retro arcade, synthwave neon, beers named like video game levels. Their website did not feel like any of that.

The old site was a stock Shopify store. On a first visit, a cookie consent banner and an 18+ age check landed on top of each other before you could see a single beer. The homepage was a promo banner, a "New in" grid and a footer. The neon palms and retro grid, the most recognisable part of the brand, only showed up as decoration at the very bottom of the page, and there was nothing on the homepage about the taproom at all.

The brewery wanted a site that sells beer easily and feels like being in their taproom. My job was to work out what that means for a website, design it, and get it built and live.

My role

Research, information architecture, UI design, building the site with AI tools, deployment

Scope

Home, Rap Taproom, Shop with cart and checkout, Info and FAQs. Desktop and mobile.

Timeline

The main build ran over two days in December 2025, after research and sketching

The Neon Raptor team in the brewery. Photo supplied by the client.

02

The approach

I started by looking at how other taproom breweries sell beer online and pull people into their bars, then pulled apart the brewery's existing site. From that I sketched a structure on paper and in Figma, and only then moved into building.

I used AI at every stage, but for different jobs. Perplexity helped me research breweries and patterns quickly. Pen and paper and Figma were for thinking. Google AI Studio did the heavy lifting on code, while I directed every decision.

What the research told me

The taproom is the product

The strongest brewery sites sell the bar as much as the beer: visit buttons, venue photos, opening hours, events.

→ Rap Taproom gets its own top-level page and a hero button

Don't make people leave the grid

Adding beer straight from the product grid keeps people browsing. Four controls per card on the old site was too much.

→ One "+" per card that adds without opening the cart

Browse the way the brewery thinks

Collection tiles work because breweries organise beer by series and range, not by filters.

→ A collections sidebar using Neon Raptor's own ranges

The brand should be the experience, not the footer

Almost every site looked like a tidy store. Neon Raptor's arcade world was their edge, and the old site hid it.

→ Interactive neon grid, a peelable logo sticker, neon type throughout

03

The design

Two ideas drove the design: make the site feel like the taproom at night, and keep the path to buying beer short and obvious.

Everything is dark, like the bar, with the brewery's neon pink doing the talking. The background is a live neon grid that tilts with your mouse and cycles through four colour moods. The type is a condensed, techy display face for headings, which reads like an arcade cabinet, with a plain sans for everything you actually need to read.

How I directed the AI

The build happened in Google AI Studio over 27 prompts across 16 and 17 December 2025. The AI wrote the code. The decisions were mine, and a lot of the work was catching what it got wrong.

These are real excerpts from the prompt log, lightly tidied for typos.

16 Dec · 13:47
Let's redesign the website for Neon Raptor brewery. Go through the website first, pick up all the information and copy it onto the new one.

Started from the brewery's real content, not placeholder copy, so the structure was designed around what they actually say.

16 Dec · 15:53
No, revert back, the layout has changed.

Asking for a full checkout broke the page layout. I rolled it back instead of patching on top of a broken state, then rebuilt the checkout in smaller steps.

16 Dec · 17:28
In the shop, when I press + don't open the cart. Add it and show the number on the cart button, then open the side panel when the cart button is clicked.

The single most important UX call in the build. The AI's default opened the cart on every add, which pulls people out of browsing. This is the "don't leave the grid" pattern from the research.

16 Dec · 17:40 and 17:47
Can you reduce the opacity of the background please? It's too bright.
You made the background opacity too low, increase it a bit.

Tuning the neon grid until it set the mood without fighting the text. Two rounds to land it.

16 Dec · 18:34 to 18:44
It's merging, separate them please. The purple thing is overlaying the logo.

Fixing layering between the header, banner and logo, and keeping the nav small while making the logo more prominent.

05

The outcome

The site is live on Netlify, with the brewery's products served from Supabase, and it's waiting to move onto their own domain.

What the brewery reported back

The team started using it and loved it. The feedback from customers ordering through it came down to two things: how easy it is to order beer, and how much it feels like being in the taproom.

That second point was the whole brief, so it was the best feedback I could have hoped for.

What I learned

AI made it possible to go from research to a working shop in days, but it doesn't have taste or context. Every useful result came from knowing what I wanted before I asked, and spotting quickly when it went wrong. The research mattered more, not less, because it gave me clear decisions to direct the build with.

Given more time

I'd add age verification into checkout, which matters for selling alcohol online in the UK. I'd photograph every beer, because some products in the shop still share one can image. And once the site is on its own domain, I'd set up analytics to see where people drop off between the product grid and payment, then test the checkout with real customers in the taproom.
